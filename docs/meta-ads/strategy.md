# Meta Ads strategy

Use this playbook to choose a campaign from customer and business evidence. Save the choices in the [campaign plan](campaign-template.md). The [brand rules](brand-rules.md) set the product, budget, creative scope, and formats. When planning a change to an existing campaign, read its saved plan and learnings first.

## 1. Establish the business goal

Verify the offer, sellable markets, stock, shipping, and destination in Shopify. Establish the result the business wants and choose the Meta objective and event that match it. For online purchase acquisition, use Sales with the verified website Purchase event. Define which orders count, the attribution basis, and how long orders take to mature.

For first-order acquisition, calculate the break-even cost per order from actual net order revenue less product cost, fulfilment, fees, and expected returns or failed deliveries. Subtract the desired first-order profit to set the acquisition target. Use repeat-order value only when observed retention, contribution, and the chosen payback period support it. Record missing costs or tracking as open decisions. Use the approved budget and remaining monthly headroom to determine how much can go to the first test.

## 2. Research and choose who to speak to

- Start with the brand's customer reviews, questions, support themes, and eligible campaign results when available. Capture what triggered the search, what the buyer wants to accomplish, what they tried instead, what almost stopped them, and the words they use. Group repeated themes, note meaningful disagreements, and record the source and strength of each finding.
- Use competitor reviews and the Meta Ad Library to see what buyers compare and what rivals promise, demonstrate, or price differently. Ad Library examples show messaging and formats. Treat audience intent inferred from an ad and any belief about its sales performance as hypotheses.
- Compare plausible buyers by the strength of the buying trigger, how well the offer answers it, the proof we can show, and whether the price and product fit. Pick the strongest supported buyer and barrier for the first campaign. If another buyer is plausible, explain what makes it secondary or worth a separate test. When there is limited evidence, choose a focused first hypothesis and state what would change the choice.
- Turn the findings into a brief: the buyer and situation, the alternative they would otherwise choose, the offer we can deliver, the benefit we can substantiate, the proof, and the main unanswered objection. Verify the offer against Shopify and capture supporting evidence for each claim.

## 3. Connect the funnel to the campaign

| Buyer stage | Job of the ad | Destination and action |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Make the need or use case recognizable and show a relevant benefit. | Send people to a page that immediately confirms the ad's promise. |
| Consideration | Demonstrate how the product works in the buyer's routine or answer a specific objection with proof. | Bring the buyer to the relevant product details or proof and make the next step clear. |
| Purchase | Explain the verified offer and resolve the last purchase concern. | Use the product page and a clear route to checkout. |

These stages describe the job of the message. Keep delivery consolidated when eligible ads share the same objective, purchase event, and market. Split delivery when a distinct goal, meaningful eligibility difference, controlled comparison, or protected test budget justifies it. Decide whether campaign-level allocation can distribute spend or an ad set needs its own budget to answer the test question. Keep targeting to serviceable markets and justified controls. Treat narrower audience settings as a test with a reason.

Evaluate retargeting by the size and relevance of eligible audiences and by whether a distinct message addresses a real objection. If the audience or budget is too small for a useful separate effort, defer it and record what would trigger reconsideration. Use only audiences permitted by the project instructions.

## 4. Choose creative before producing it

Compare three signals when available: actual customer language, performance of eligible ads by concept and format, and relevant competitor or organic creative. Rank ideas by the evidence for the buying barrier, the strength of available proof, and how much the next test would teach. Choose distinct reasons to buy before making several versions of the same hook. Keep promising alternatives in the campaign notes for later.

Record the buyer stage, purchase reason, proof, and test for each concept. Hand these decisions to the [creative workflow](creative.md) for asset selection, adaptation, and delivery.

## 5. Set the test and hand it off

Decide which uncertainty matters most to the next business decision: the buyer, message, offer, destination, or delivery. Use the [testing method](optimize.md#test) to define the comparison and the evidence required. Estimate affordable result volume from a documented cost estimate when available. When evidence or budget is limited, keep the test small enough to learn from and record when the result will be directional. Give the campaign one primary outcome. Use intermediate funnel metrics to diagnose why it moved.

Record the selection rationale, evidence, unresolved assumptions, and what would change the plan. Verify purchase tracking and preflight the assets when launch preparation is in scope. Use the [runbook](runbook.md) for execution and the [decision playbook](optimize.md) for the next review.
